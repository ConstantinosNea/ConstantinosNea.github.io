Heat waves are no longer rare events. They are a predictable public health stress test — on bodies, hospitals, and neighbors.

## Know the difference

**Heat exhaustion** often looks like heavy sweating, weakness, dizziness, and nausea. **Heat stroke** is a medical emergency: confusion, hot/dry or somehow still-sweaty skin, and a body temperature that keeps climbing.

When in doubt, cool the person and call for emergency help.

## What actually helps

1. Check on neighbors who live alone or lack air conditioning
2. Drink water before you feel thirsty
3. Shift outdoor work and exercise to cooler hours
4. Know your local cooling center

Prevention is community infrastructure, not just individual grit.

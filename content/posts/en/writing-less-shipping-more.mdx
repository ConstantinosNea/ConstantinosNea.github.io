Clarity is a force multiplier. The same discipline that turns a sprawling draft into a sharp essay can turn a bloated feature into something people actually finish using.

## Start with the reader

Before opening an editor—whether for prose or code—ask who this is for and what they need to leave knowing or able to do. If you cannot answer in one sentence, you are not ready to write.

That single sentence becomes your north star. Cut anything that does not serve it.

## Prefer specificity over volume

Vague claims hide weak thinking. Prefer:

- Concrete examples over abstractions
- Named trade-offs over absolute rules
- Short paragraphs that earn the next one

> The goal is not to sound smart. The goal is to make the next decision easier for someone else.

## Ship the smallest complete thought

A published half-page that stands alone beats an unfinished opus. Treat each post like a pull request: scoped, reviewable, and mergeable today.

When you sit down to write your next piece, try this constraint: **one idea, one audience, one call to action.** Everything else can wait for the next post.

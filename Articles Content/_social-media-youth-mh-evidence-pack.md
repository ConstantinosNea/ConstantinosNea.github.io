## Synthesis
- Europe: rise in **problematic** SMU (7→11%), girls higher; most adolescents are active/intense without meeting problematic threshold.
- Mechanisms of concern (sleep, body image/social comparison, cyberbullying, compulsive use) have convergent associative evidence; benefits (connection, support for some groups) also documented.
- “Rewiring” / primary-cause headlines outrun the causal evidence (NASEM, OECD).
- Public-health stance: distinguish use patterns; protect against known harms; avoid blanket “social media is bad.”

## Conflicts / limits
- HBSC associations with well-being often cited from prior research in WHO news (linked, not always new causal estimates in Vol.6).
- US 3-hour “double risk” is one adjusted longitudinal study — report as association with context.
- Do not claim population-level causal “rewiring.”
